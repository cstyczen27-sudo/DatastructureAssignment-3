Stacks are best for undo/redo because, when you tell the program to undo the 
last action, it needs to treat that action as the most recent one. 
Then those actions go into the undo stack; once an action is undone, 
it goes into the redo stack so you can redo it with the redo command. 
A queue is better for a help desk for the same reason it is useful in the 
real world: it keeps things organized and straightforward. 
One person needs help, so they enter the queue; then the next, and the next, 
and so on. Then, when you handle the first person in line, it removes that 
person once helped, moves the second person up, and repeats. 
My implementation of linked-list stacks and queues, at least in my opinion, 
once I got the hang of it, they were easier to navigate and use. 
Instead of having to tell a program to be flexible and aware of what could 
potentially be in a list at certain indexes, you can instead say Here is a 
list of objects that all share XYZ. You are looking for objects that match 
X but only have Z. Start at the beginning, then go through the whole linked 
list and put everything that matches into this queue so that I can sort through 
those myself.
